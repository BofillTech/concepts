The harbour year

The party is downstairs. So is breakfast.

Shelter Cove Harbour was laid out to feel like a Mediterranean village, and in season it is the busiest stretch of the island. Seven harbour-front restaurants, the King Neptune sundial, and a calendar that changes character four times a year.

  1. March → May

    Music & Taste on the Harbour

    A weekly street dance party beneath the larger-than-life King Neptune sundial. The island's most popular dance bands play; the seven harbour-front restaurants take turns serving light fare and cocktails from kiosks alongside. Bring your own chair, or take a first-come seat at any of the restaurants.

  2. Mid-June → mid-August

    Harbourfest, and the stage moves next door

    The restaurants pull back to their own patio musicians and the big party shifts to the centre stage right beside the building. Monday through Friday, Shannon Tanner plays his family show — kids limbo lower, then take the mic. Thursday he brings the full band; Shan Tan and the Oyster Reefers close out Parrot Palooza at sunset. Bouncy houses, face painting, hair wrapping, crafts, and a little train that runs the length of the basin. Tuesday ends with fireworks.

  3. Mid-Aug → October

    The quieter half of summer

    Families head back to school and the island changes gear. The intensity is over but the season isn't — this is the leisurely stretch along the harbour and the beach. The first fall breezes make it prime golf weather, and island businesses line up the festivals: Wingfest, Oyster Festival, Seafood Festival, the Chili Cook-off, Cajun Festival and Winefest.

  4. The holidays

    Lights on the water

    The harbour lights up one more time. Not fireworks this round — the focus moves to the boats, where owners spend the month out-doing each other with strands of lights.

You don't have to stand in the crowd for the fireworks. From the right balcony, you get the whole display.

Beach & trails

A mile to the sand. Three miles of it private.

We sit in the centre of the island, one mile from twelve miles of beach — and guests get access to three miles of private Palmetto Dunes shore. In season the free Dunes Buggy shuttle runs door-to-door through Palmetto Dunes and Shelter Cove Harbour: the Towne Centre, Kroger, the community parks, the restaurants and boutiques, and best of all, the beach.

Ownership

Not a timeshare. A deeded quarter.

Why manage an island home fifty-two weeks a year when you can't be here that often? Each Harbourside III villa has four separately deeded owners, with no joint liability between them.

Ownership opportunities

  • A recorded deeded interest in real estate — title in perpetuity, not an expiring leasehold.
  • Use it, rent it, lend it, will it or sell it. Weeks you can't use can go into the rental pool.
  • A quarter conveys six two-week intervals plus one single week: thirteen weeks a year.
  • Quarters rotate by one week annually, so every four years you've owned every week number.
