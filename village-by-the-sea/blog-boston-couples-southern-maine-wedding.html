Why Boston Couples Are Choosing
Southern Maine for Their Wedding

A destination wedding an hour and a half from home

Village by the Sea · 2026-09-01 · 6 min read

Boston has no shortage of wedding venues. It also has Boston prices, Boston parking, and a Saturday in June where three other weddings are happening within a mile of yours. More and more couples are looking eighty miles north instead, to Wells, Maine, and deciding the drive is worth it.

The drive is shorter than people expect

Wells sits on Route 1 in Southern Maine, about seventy-five miles from Boston, roughly an hour and a half by car with no tolls to speak of once you're past the city. That's closer than plenty of venues couples already consider "local," the Berkshires, the Cape, the Hamptons, and it's a straight shot up I-95 rather than a summer-traffic crawl.

For guests flying in, Boston Logan is the easiest airport to route through, and from there it's the same hour and a half by car or a rental. Portsmouth, New Hampshire and its small regional airport sit even closer, about thirty-five minutes south of Wells, which is worth mentioning to any out-of-town family trying to book flights.

What a Maine wedding weekend actually costs

Venue pricing in Boston proper for a comparable guest count often starts well above what a full wedding weekend costs at Village by the Sea, and that's before you've booked a single hotel room block for your guests. Here, the ceremony pergola, the covered terrace, the Maine Ballroom and the guest accommodations are all part of the same property, which means one contract instead of three, and no shuttle budget between a ceremony venue, a reception venue and a hotel.

Our wedding packages start at For Always and run through For Eternity, each built around a plated or buffet dinner, a coordinator, and the space itself. Because everything happens on the same eleven acres, couples tend to spend less on the logistics that don't show up in any photograph, transportation, extra venue rental days, vendor travel fees, and more on the parts their guests will actually remember.

A weekend, not just a Saturday

A Boston venue typically rents you a room for an afternoon. A Maine wedding weekend at Village by the Sea gives your guests a place to arrive Thursday or Friday, unpack into a real condominium suite with a kitchen and a living room, and stay through Sunday brunch. Nobody is driving home after the last dance. Nobody is splitting the wedding party across three different hotels ten minutes apart in different directions.

That difference matters more than it sounds like it should. Guests who are already committing a full weekend to your wedding tend to enjoy it more when the weekend itself feels like a trip, not a single obligation squeezed between a Friday flight and a Monday meeting. Wells Beach, Ogunquit's Marginal Way and Kennebunkport's Dock Square are all a short drive away, giving out-of-town guests something to do with a Saturday afternoon or a Sunday morning that isn't just waiting around.

Who this actually works for

Not every couple wants a destination wedding. But if your guest list already skews toward Massachusetts, New Hampshire and the rest of New England, "destination" might be overstating it, this is closer to asking everyone to take a slightly longer Saturday drive in exchange for a wedding weekend that doesn't end the moment the reception does.

It tends to appeal most to couples who want the ceremony, the party and the guest rooms handled by one team instead of three, and who would rather their money show up in the room than in a shuttle contract.
