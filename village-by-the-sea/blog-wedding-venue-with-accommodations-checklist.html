What to Look for in a
Wedding Venue With Accommodations

A practical checklist before you sign

Village by the Sea · 2026-09-01 · 6 min read

"Venue with accommodations" gets used loosely in wedding marketing. Some venues mean there's a hotel next door with a discount code. Others mean guests are genuinely staying on the same property as the ceremony. The difference matters more than it sounds like it should, and it's worth asking specific questions before you sign anything.

How far is "on site," actually?

Ask whether guest accommodations are on the same property as the ceremony and reception, or a shuttle ride away. At Village by the Sea, the suites, the pergola and the Maine Ballroom sit on the same eleven acres, guests walk, they don't get bussed. That's not universal. Get the actual walking distance, not the marketing description of it.

What kind of room are your guests actually getting?

A standard hotel room and a real suite are different products. Ask what's in the room: is there a kitchen, a separate living room, more than one bathroom? Families traveling with kids, older relatives, or a full bridal party benefit enormously from a suite over a single hotel room, there's somewhere for people to spread out, somewhere for a baby to nap, somewhere to cook breakfast instead of hunting for a diner at 7 a.m.

Who's on the hook for the room block?

This is the question couples skip most often. Some venues require you to guarantee a minimum number of room-nights, meaning you're financially responsible if guests don't book enough rooms. Ask directly whether the block costs you anything up front. A group room block here is created at no cost to you, and guests book and pay for their own suites.

Can you do more than one event there?

If you're hoping for a rehearsal dinner, a welcome reception, or a farewell breakfast, ask whether the venue has a separate space for it, and whether that space is included or a separate booking with a separate vendor. Having the York Room available for a rehearsal dinner the night before the wedding, in the same building as the ballroom, is part of what turns a single day into a full weekend.

What happens if it rains?

Any outdoor ceremony space needs a real indoor backup, not a tent rental scramble two days before your wedding. Ask to see the actual indoor space they'd move you to, and whether it's included in your package or an add-on.

A short checklist

  • Guest rooms on the same property, not a shuttle away
  • Suites with kitchens and separate living space
  • No financial risk on the room block
  • A separate space for rehearsal dinners or welcome events
  • A real indoor ceremony backup
  • One team handling ceremony, reception and lodging

If a venue can answer all six of those clearly, "wedding venue with accommodations" means what it sounds like it should.
