Our Story

Run by the family that lives here

William and Jamie Welborn own and operate The Rustic Acre, and they live on-site. That means a real person — not a call center — is around to help with a late check-in, a trail recommendation, or a lost pair of hiking boots. William grew up in this valley, so the tips you get on where to watch elk or which trailhead to skip on a busy Saturday come from a lifetime here, not a guidebook.

The seven units share the same 650 S. Saint Vrain Ave. address but each has its own name, its own layout, and its own personality — more like a small mountain hamlet than a hotel wing.

Bring the whole crew

Dogs are family here too

The Welborns don't just tolerate dogs — they also own Estes Park Pet Supply and Estes Paws down the road, so a four-legged guest gets a genuinely warm welcome, not a grudging exception. Most units take up to two dogs for $30/night plus tax, arranged through the guest portal after you book.

Every unit is its own private space, so you can wake up for breakfast whenever you like, spread out with friends or family, and come and go on your own schedule — no shared hallways, no fixed breakfast hours, just your own front door a few miles from the park.

Around the Property

A closer look

Location

Five miles from the park, one mile from downtown

The Rustic Acre sits in the enviable stretch between historic downtown Estes Park and the entrances to Rocky Mountain National Park — close enough to reach both without fighting summer traffic through town.

  • 1 miWalkable, bikeable route into downtown Estes Park — about a 20 minute walk.
  • 5 miDrive to the Beaver Meadows entrance of Rocky Mountain National Park.
  • 6 miDrive to the Fall River entrance — take Hwy 7 or the Moccasin Circle bypass to dodge summer traffic.
Good to Know

A few things guests ask

Self check-in, any time

The Rustic Acre runs on self check-in, so a late arrival is never a problem — check-in details are sent before you arrive.

Park entry reservations

During peak season (late May through mid-October), Rocky Mountain National Park requires a Timed Entry Permit, booked through recreation.gov.

Cool mountain air, mostly no A/C

Two units (Mt. Olympus and Prospect Mountain) have air conditioning; the rest rely on Estes Park's naturally cool evenings and in-room fans.

Every unit has a kitchenette or kitchen

All units include at least a fridge or mini-fridge, and most include a full kitchen for cooking in after a day on the trail.
