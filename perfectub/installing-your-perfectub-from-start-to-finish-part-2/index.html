Installing Your Perfectub, From Start to Finish Part II

By Jerry GouldJune 5, 2026

The Rundown

  • Level the tub footprint (laser + shims) so alignment and finishes stay predictable
  • Create full, even support under the tub base using the method that fits your build and Brak Tub’s instructions
  • Dry-fit the drain route before any PVC glue to avoid rework
  • Choose fittings based on slab access and risk of leak, not just convenience

Using some methods and lessons from the video on Stud Pack’s channel, this part will help make your installation process very smooth.

The Stud Pack crew approaches this installation as experienced installers, going through an excellent demonstration of fitting the tub with plumbing pierces and a lot of details on the piping he uses. We will break those down here to help you in your installation.

1. Level the Tub Footprint Before Plumbing Work

The goal here is for your tub to sit right so your drain path and finish work stay predictable. To do this:

  • Set up a laser and identify your high and low points across the tub footprint.
  • Measure the differences and build shims to match what your slab actually does. In the video, they call out shims like 1/16 and ¼ inch.
  • Preventing water from sitting in low spots and causing discoloration over time
  • Keeping your tile lines clean so you don’t end up with tapered cuts
  • Keeping your drain alignment consistent once you start fitting plumbing
  • Reducing the chance you’ll need last-minute adjustments after dry fitting

When you get the leveling done right, you’ll not need to worry about alignment later.

PLEASE NOTE: If the floor that the tub will be placed on is leveled and as substantial as 3/8′ plywood subfloor, this step will not be needed. If this is the case, please proceed to step three.

2. Set the Tub on Full Support

Once you’ve gotten the shims right, lock the plan in. In the video, they use a cement-based setting approach for their slab. For your situation, follow Brak Tub’s installation instructions.

When getting ready to prep, apply thinset or a wet mortar bed or foam bedding (which Brak Tub recommends) under the tub’s defined support areas, not just the corners, so that the weight is distributed properly. Creating this stable base will reduce flex, noise, and long-term movement.

3. Check the Drain Path With a Dry Fit

Before gluing the PVC, check how the drain route lines up with what’s already in the slab. You want to confirm alignment at this stage, which is why this is necessary.

You should also account for the overflow connection during planning. In the video, they identify the overflow connection size as 1-½ inch [VERIFY], which is code.

Confirming the connection path while you still have room to adjust will save you a lot of time and headache, especially when the access gets tight.

4. Choose Fittings Based on Access and Leak Risk

Consider various fitting options if you want to build a connection you trust after the tub is set. As demonstrated in the video, certain choices make life easier when you’re connecting to a slab rough-in that has limited access.

The important lesson isn’t about getting a single right fitting. It’s about choosing parts that give you workable angles and clean connections without overcomplicating the run. In slab situations, your fitting choices can either save you an hour or cost you a day.

Work With Brak Tub

If you want the installation to feel straightforward, your decisions need to be locked in before you’re fighting tight clearances and glued joints. That’s why we place emphasis on having your layout, drain, orientation, and rough-in plan settled while the changes are still easy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need an access opening for the drain connections?

It’s strongly recommended. We suggest providing an additional opening for access to the drain components, which makes dry-fitting and final connections far easier.

Is the Perfectub meant to sit in a mortar bed, or can it be self-supporting?

The tub is self-supporting and must be installed on a smooth level sub-floor. The floor should be supported and level in BOTH DIRECTIONS and on each board (back, middle and front). The use of a wet mortar bed or foam bedding is not required, but highly recommended when the floor is uneven or older, in the case of most remodeling jobs.

Should the tub rim carry weight while you level and secure it?

No. The rim must not support weight. The base needs proper support and shimming so that the tub isn’t hung by the flange or rim.

Do I still need plumber’s putty and silicone if the waste/overflow is built in?

Yes. Integrated doesn’t mean “no sealing,” rather, it means fewer parts to source. Materials like plumber’s putty and silicone sealant are part of the installation toolkit, even though the tub system is integrated.
