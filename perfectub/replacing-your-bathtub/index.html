Replacing Your Bathtub

By Jerry GouldJuly 2, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • When replacing a bathtub, you’ll be dealing with demolition, wall repairs, waterproofing, and fitting considerations.
  • The replacement costs can include labor, materials, plumbing work, and hidden damage.
  • A new tub should be chosen for comfort, durability, fit, and installation needs.
  • Choose a tub that offers predictable installation and avoid those with complicated workflows.

Removing and replacing a bathtub is one of the more ambitious renovation projects that you can undertake as an expert or homeowner. This is because bathtub replacement s always affect everything from walls and plumbing to the finished walls and floors at the same time.

Even as just a homeowner, you should have an idea about replacing a tub and shower in your house, how that affects your wall system and finished enclosure, and also better installation options that are available on the market today, so you can use your space better and make any future renovations easier.

Let’s discuss all that in this article.

Why Replace a Bathtub?

There are several reasons why any homeowner would think about replacing a bathtub in their house. The reasons can range from visible wear, such as chips and cracks, to getting rid of a tub that feels cramped or doesn’t efficiently use the alcove space.

Those and so many other reasons push people every day into finally deciding to get rid of their old bathtubs, but basically, if you feel that you don’t like your bathtub in any way, that’s reason enough for you to consider replacing it.

Important Considerations When Replacing a Tub and Shower

Planning and Budgeting

Before you start demolishing, take time to understand what goes into replacing a bathtub.

Beyond the cost of the new fixture, your bathtub replacement budget may need to cover wall removal, plumbing reconnection, subfloor repairs, finish materials, labor, and waterproofing work.

Measuring the space is also important. Many bathrooms use a standard 60×30 alcove, but not every tub is designed the same way within that footprint, so you shouldn’t plan these replacements using dimensions alone, but the installation requirements as well.

Therefore, take time to understand what the old tub is hiding and what the new tub will require.

Choosing Your New Bathtub

When replacing a bathtub, keep in mind that material, structure, and design details all affect how the tub performs during installation and daily use. Some bathtub options you might want to consider include:

  • Acrylic: It’s a popular option for daily use because it offers durability, heat retention, and a more comfortable bathing experience than many lower-grade alternatives.
  • Fiberglass: These have an initial lower cost, but they are often less durable and less satisfying over time.

You also want to check the weight of the tub because heavier options can cause structural issues and make handling it a nightmare.

When replacing a bath tub and shower, you want to strike a balance between comfort, durability, and installation practicality rather than appearance alone.

Removing the Old Tub

From cutting through caulk to opening the wall area around the flange and disconnecting the drain and overflow, this step should also be considered and done very carefully to avoid damaging other finishes.

Installing the New Tub

For a successful bathtub replacement, make sure the subfloor is level, the drain is correctly aligned, and there is a clean transition between the tub and the wall system.

Some tub designs require extra support materials beneath the base, which adds time and materials when replacing a bathtub. All of these issues can make the job labor-intensive, which is something you should avoid as much as possible.

Instead, go for a tub that supports a more predictable installation rather than adding avoidable complications to the workflow.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes wall integration important when replacing a bathtub?

This transition between the tub and the surrounding wall system affects waterproofing, finish quality, and long-term performance. That’s one reason the Perfectub has a patented back edge that smoothly integrates with the tile walls.

What is the primary benefit of the Perfectub compared to standard acrylic tubs?

Your bathroom design already accommodates a standard 30 x 60 inch tub, you might as well get a tub that offers about 30% more floor space. It features a reinforced, built-in solid base that usually means less prep and fewer messy bedding materials during installation, providing a rock-solid feel without the extra labor. The use of a wet mortar bed or foam bedding is not required, but highly recommended when the floor is uneven or older, in the case of most remodeling jobs.

How much time does a built-in solid base save during a remodel?

On a smooth, level subfloor, skipping a mortar bed can save several hours of active labor and the overnight curing wait time. On uneven or older floors, a wet mortar bed or foam bedding is still highly recommended.
