Do You Need a Bathtub For Resale Value? Here’s What Homeowners Should Know

By Bruce AhmesMarch 6, 2026

The Rundown

  • Not having a bathtub cuts out a huge part of your buyer pool.
  • Homes without one often get pushed into the “¾ bath” category online.
  • Many buyers want a tub for comfort or a place to decompress.
  • Modern tubs solve accessibility concerns without hurting resale.

Answers to questions like “ Are bathtubs still popular?”, “ Do you need a bathtub for resale value?” “ Should you have a bathtub in your house?” or “ bath vs shower pros and cons ” come up any time a homeowner thinks about upgrading a bathroom or reshaping the layout entirely.

These questions cut straight to what future buyers expect and what might quietly reduce your pool of interested buyers before anyone even steps inside the house.

And in today’s market, where families, long-term planners, and wellness-focused buyers still shape much of the demand, the presence of at least one bathtub plays a larger role than homeowners often realize. Here is what you should know.

The “Alienation” Risk

For families with kids, parents might want a bathtub for bathing the babies and toddlers. You do need a bathtub for resale value in this case. One of the things that these families will be looking for as a non-negotiable tool will be a bathtub.

Now, if you don’t have one, those families will rule you out immediately. And because they represent one of the largest active buyer groups in most markets, removing the tub shrinks your pool of potential buyers with one decision.

The One-Tub Rule

In many places, a bathroom without a tub is treated as a “three-quarter bath,” which changes how the listing appears in search filters. This is why you should have a bathtub in your house.

For it to be considered a full bath, you do need a bathtub, not just for resale value, but even for the visibility of your listing, since those houses without bathtubs are categorized differently from those with bathtubs.

It doesn’t mean that you agonize over the endless bath vs shower pros and cons or stress about which to put where. You don’t need a tub in every bathroom to preserve the value. But you do need at least one somewhere in the house to make it usable for a wide range of buyers and stop you from falling into the “¾ bath” trap without realizing it.

A Bathtub as a Wellness Sanctuary

Many people want a room that doubles as a place to decompress, and a deep soaking tub does that. You do need a bathtub to appeal to this group of buyers, which may include the aging-in-place parents.

When these buyers walk into a bathroom without a tub, they see a room with no place to retreat. If they like the house a lot, they might talk themselves into getting the bathtub later, but as a homeowner, you want to lower the friction that potential buyers might have towards making that purchase decision.

A Tub That’s Future Proof

Some homeowners think that they need to remove the bathtub to make the space easier to use as they get older, but if you do need a bathtub for the resale value, some modern tub designs, like the Perfectub from Brak Tub, mean that you don’t have to get rid of the bathtub

Instead of forcing people to choose between a more accessible option and long-term resale, it bridges that gap. Frequently Asked Questions

Do I actually need a tub in the main bath?

Not really. Most agents only stress having one somewhere in the house. The main bath can be shower-only if another tub exists.

If I only have one bathroom, can I skip the tub?

Since, in many cases, you do need a bathtub for the resale value, that’s where you might run into trouble. Losing the only tub shuts the door on a lot of buyers. Don’t skip it if you can invest in a tub.

Is acrylic okay for resale?

Yes. Most buyers don’t complain about acrylic. High-end materials just change the vibe, not the baseline value.

How do people make their tub feel nicer without remodeling?

Little things: deeper water, softer lighting, cleaner styling, comfortable back rest, more elbow room, shelf or caddy nearby, more accessible, and shower-chair-friendly options. Those details make the space better.
