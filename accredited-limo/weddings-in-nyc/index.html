Wedding Transportation in NYC & Westchester: Moving Your Bridal Party and Guests

Plan wedding-day transportation in Westchester and NYC: sedans and SUVs for the couple, Sprinters for the bridal party, and minibuses or coaches for guests.

A wedding day runs on timing. Hair and makeup finish late, the photographer wants twenty more minutes at the park, and 120 guests need to get from the hotel to the ceremony and on to the reception — on schedule, in their best clothes, without anyone hunting for parking.

That is where professional group transportation earns its place. At Accredited Limousine Service, wedding work is mostly about moving people well: the couple, the bridal party, family, and guests, between every stop of the day. Here is how to plan it.

Start with the day's timeline, not the vehicle

Before choosing vehicles, write down every movement of the day:

  1. Getting ready — where the couple and wedding party are dressing (hotel, home, venue suite).
  2. First look and photos — any park, waterfront, or city stops your photographer has planned.
  3. Ceremony — arrival time, and how early the wedding party needs to be there.
  4. Guest arrivals — which hotels guests are staying at and when they need to be seated.
  5. Ceremony to reception — whether it is the same venue or a separate drive.
  6. End of night — guest returns to hotels, and the couple's departure.

For each movement, note who is riding, how many people, pickup address, drop-off address, and the time they must arrive. Our dispatch team builds the schedule around this list, adds buffer for traffic, and assigns the right vehicle to each leg.

Which vehicle for whom

The couple: sedan or SUV

For the couple's own rides — to the ceremony, between photo locations, and the final departure — a late-model Volvo S90 sedan or a Lincoln Aviator or Chevrolet Suburban SUV is the right fit. They are quiet, comfortable, and roomy enough for a gown, with a professional chauffeur handling doors, timing, and route. SUVs are also a good choice for parents and grandparents who want a private ride.

The bridal party: Mercedes Sprinter

A Mercedes Sprinter keeps the wedding party together, which photographers and planners love: everyone arrives at the same moment, ready for the next shot.

  • Standard Sprinter — straightforward, comfortable group seating for the wedding party.
  • Luxury / executive Sprinter (up to 12 passengers) — upgraded seating, interior lighting, and extra headroom for gowns, suits, and bouquets.

Sprinters are ideal for the getting-ready-to-ceremony run, photo stops around Westchester or Manhattan, and the ride to the reception.

Guests: minibuses and motor coaches

Guest shuttles are often the most valuable part of a wedding transportation plan. Instead of dozens of guests navigating, parking, and arriving at different times, one or two vehicles bring everyone together.

  • Minibuses (24 and 36 passengers) — well suited to mid-size weddings or a single hotel block.
  • Motor coaches (40 and 56 passengers) — for larger guest lists, with comfortable reclining seating; select coaches include an onboard restroom, PA system, and climate control.

Shuttles can run on a fixed loop between hotels and the venue, or make scheduled departures before the ceremony and at the end of the night. Out-of-town guests appreciate not having to figure out the area, and everyone gets home safely after the reception.

Sample plan for a 150-guest Westchester wedding

Leg Riders Vehicle
Hotel → ceremony (getting ready) Couple (separately) 2 × sedan or SUV
Hotel → photos → ceremony 10 bridal party Luxury Sprinter
Hotel → ceremony Parents & grandparents SUV
Hotel → ceremony → reception → hotel 100+ guests 2 × 56-passenger coach (or coach + 36-passenger minibus)
End of night Couple Sedan or SUV

Every wedding is different. Dispatch will recommend the right mix based on headcount, venue access, and timing.

Booking tips

  • Book early. Spring and fall Saturdays fill quickly, especially for Sprinters and coaches. Reserve as soon as your venue and date are set.
  • Share a final headcount and guest hotel list about two weeks out so we can confirm vehicle sizes.
  • Check venue access. Some estates and city venues have tight drives or restricted curb space for coaches; we confirm drop-off points in advance.
  • Build in buffer. We plan around NYC and Westchester traffic, but photo stops always take longer than expected.
  • Name a day-of contact — a planner, coordinator, or trusted family member — who can reach dispatch directly.
  • Plan the last ride first. Late-night guest returns are the leg most often overlooked.
