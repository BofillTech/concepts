Condo & Co-op Hotel Resources

A resource hub for general managers and boards at condo-hotels and co-op resorts: practical notes on the operational choices that affect occupancy and revenue. QuillStay works with properties in this space every day. We'll add more tips here as they come up.

Tip 1

Don't lock guests into a specific room number, or charge a nightly room lock fee if they insist

Locking a guest or owner into a requested room number makes it much harder to fill the calendar. You cannot move reservations around to fit a longer stay, so you leave money on the table.

Example: a five-room, two-bedroom room type (rooms 2, 3, 4, 6 and 7)

  • Rooms 6 and 7 are already fully booked for the dates shown. The other rooms have some short stays.
  • A guest calls and wants to arrive Thursday. The calendar still shows one room available on each night, Thursday through Monday, so the nights are there.
  • If those existing reservations are not locked to a specific room, the front desk can slide the short stays around ("playing Tetris") and place the new guest in one room for the whole stay.
  • If every reservation is locked to the room the guest asked for, those stays cannot be moved. Even though the nights are open, the Thursday arrival may have to change rooms each night.

That is why locking people into a specific room number, just because they asked, hurts the bottom line and total hotel revenue. You lose the flexibility to take those last reservations.

If a guest or owner insists on a specific room, charge a nightly room lock fee.

For example, $25 per night. It should be high enough that a room lock is a deliberate choice, not the default.
