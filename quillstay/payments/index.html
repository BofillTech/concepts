How a payment actually moves

Gateway and processor are two different jobs

It's a common mix-up, so here's the short version: the gateway is the secure pipe that carries the card data from your booking engine to the card networks. The processor is who actually moves the money and deposits it in your bank account. QuillStay connects both automatically.

Guest checks out

A guest enters their card on your QuillStay booking page, or your front desk keys it in at check-in.

Fluid Pay — the gateway

The card is securely authorized and tokenized, then passed to the card networks and your processor for approval.

Host Merchant Services — the processor

Your merchant account settles the transaction and deposits funds into your bank, typically the next business day.

The two providers, in plain terms

What each one does for your property

Payment Gateway

Fluid Pay

PCI DSS Level 1 · partner-only, never boards merchants directly

Fluid Pay is the secure connection between your QuillStay booking engine and the card networks — it authorizes, tokenizes, and routes every transaction without ever putting raw card data through your own systems. It's built specifically for software platforms like QuillStay, running on modern cloud infrastructure rather than legacy, on-premise gateway hardware.

  • PCI DSS Level 1 service provider
  • Card tokenization keeps guest card data out of QuillStay's own systems
  • EMV, contactless, and card-not-present transactions all run through the same connection
  • Built-in fraud detection tuned to the transaction, not generic rules

Payment Processor

Host Merchant Services

Registered ISO of PNC Bank, Synovus Bank & Wells Fargo · founded 2010

Host Merchant Services sets up and manages the actual merchant account behind your property — the relationship with the banks that move money from a guest's card into your account.
