1950s How It Began

“Isn’t this beautiful?”

Lydia Gumbs loved the ocean. One day in the early 1950s, relaxing on a trip to the Jersey shore, she turned to her husband Jeremiah and said, “Isn’t this beautiful?” “This is nice,” he replied, “but not quite as beautiful as where I come from.”

From that moment, Lydia made it her mission to see her husband’s home country of Anguilla for herself. Jeremiah was reluctant — he told her of an undeveloped place where he had written his math homework in the sand, because the schools could not afford paper. Where would they even stay?

Pregnant and afraid of heights, Lydia went anyway. The journey took several airplanes and boats and overnight stops on larger islands. When she finally arrived, she was astounded by the beauty of the land, sea and sky. “I thought I went to heaven and didn’t die,” she told everyone who would listen.

Sacred Ground

A shore for renewing the soul

When Lydia said Anguilla was a place where people would come to renew their souls, she spoke from her own experience — but she may also have sensed something far older. Anguilla is an ancient coral reef that grew in the mouth of a volcano, unlike anywhere else in the region.

Rendezvous Bay in particular has been a place of ceremony and renewal for thousands of years. The indigenous Arawak traveled here by canoe to sustain their spirits, and left sacred fossils behind in the sand.

The Work

Lydia had the vision.
Jeremiah made it real.

In the 1950s, Anguilla had no commercial travel routes and no beach hotels. It didn’t matter. For Lydia, the beauty of Anguilla had once seemed impossible too — until she saw it for herself. Living between Anguilla and New Jersey, she and Jeremiah set out to build Rendezvous Bay Hotel, the island’s first beachfront hotel.

Jeremiah had the work ethic to make the impossible real. He gathered the land, cleared the road, built a sea wall of stones and shells, and raised the first concrete buildings — later founding the airline Gumbs Air to bring visitors to the island. In his letters home he asked Lydia to send supplies, and pleaded with her to somehow send rain, for the island was prone to drought, and there is no concrete without water.

And because there is no Anguilla without empowered Anguillians, the couple helped found the Anguilla Benevolent Society in the New York tri-state area, sending building materials, school supplies and books back to the island’s schools.

Through the Years

From the family album

1958 to Today

God’s Bathtub

In 1958, Rendezvous Bay Hotel broke ground and launched the tourism business that has become Anguilla’s primary industry. Since then it has been a meeting place for the visionaries of the Anguillian revolution, a home for artist retreats and family reunions, and the beach where countless couples have said their vows. For almost seventy years, families have come to rest and reconnect in this laid-back, family-owned haven — where the doors open straight onto what Condé Nast has called the most beautiful beach in the world.
