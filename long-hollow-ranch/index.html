The Ranch

Some places tell you their story. This one lived it.

1890

A homestead in Long Hollow Canyon

‘Lige’ Sparks homesteaded part of Long Hollow Canyon in the late 19th century, forever tying his legacy to the ranch. The Bunkhouse — the first building on the property — dates to the 1800s and still stands today.

1902

The Black Butte Land & Livestock Company

At its peak the company ran 2,200 head of cattle and over 10,000 sheep across five ranches, with a summer range from Sparks Lake in the high Cascades to the Metolius River. Long Hollow was its headquarters — a self-sufficient operation that produced its own meat, eggs, milk, butter, even soap. Around 1,000 sheep a day were sheared here every June.

1905

The Ranch House rises

Built as the company’s main headquarters, the Ranch House still anchors the property. The original 1800s building became a bunkhouse for cowboys, and the Homestead House served as the general store — supplying dry goods, clothing, and repair parts for wagons and teams. The blacksmith shop and the draft-horse stalls in the main barn survive to this day.

1918

The Holmes Ranch years

When the company dissolved, foreman A.S. Holmes received part of Long Hollow — including the ranch buildings — as his share. The Holmes family ranched here for decades, hosting the community’s 4th of July picnics until 1930. One of Central Oregon’s last traditional cattle drives rode out in 1960.

1990

Brought back to life

The Bloofeldt family purchased the land in the early 1990s and spent more than twenty years restoring it — transforming Long Hollow into one of the first dude ranches in Central Oregon while preserving its history for future generations of visitors.

Today

A working ranch, open to you

Long Hollow is a thriving cattle ranch with its own herd — and a place for weekend weddings, family reunions, dude ranch weeks, bachelorette getaways, corporate retreats, birthday celebrations, or a relaxing staycation. The blacksmith shop now houses the goats and chickens, and our friendly horses are always ready to say hello.
