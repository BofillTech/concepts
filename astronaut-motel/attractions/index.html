Out the doorRight here in the Crest.

Start with what’s already yours: the beach out front and a pool deck that looks right over it.

The beach Free

Directly in front of the motel

Wide, soft sand on the oceanfront with a southern exposure. The Wildwoods’ beaches don’t require beach tags, so just grab a chair and go.

Our pool deck

On property

The oceanfront pool with its sliding board, a separate fenced kiddie pool, the rooftop deck and barbecue grills for the end of a beach day.

See the pool deck

Up the islandThe Wildwoods.

The boardwalk, the piers and the neon that made this island famous in the 1950s and ’60s, all a short drive or tram ride north.

The Wildwood boardwalk

Wildwood and North Wildwood

The heart of a Wildwoods vacation: rides, arcades, boardwalk food and shops. Ride the iconic Sightseer Tram Cars along the boards instead of walking the whole way.

Good to know: the tram cars come up behind you on the boardwalk, so listen for their famous warning.

The Wildwoods visitor guide

Morey’s Piers and Waterparks

On the Wildwood boardwalk

A family-owned boardwalk institution since 1969, with three amusement piers (Surfside, Mariner’s and Adventure) and two beachfront waterparks, Ocean Oasis and Raging Waters. The refurbished Giant Wheel is back on the skyline.

Good to know: the season opens in early May. Buy ride and waterpark passes online ahead of time.

Morey’s Piers tickets and hours

Doo Wop Experience Museum Free

4500 Ocean Avenue, Wildwood

A 1950s diner rescued from Wildwood Crest and turned into a museum of neon signs, music and mid-century design, with a neon sign garden outside. Named one of the nation’s best pop culture museums by USA Today readers four years running.

Good to know: Back to the ’50s Neon Night trolley tours leave from the museum on Tuesday and Thursday nights in summer.

Doo Wop Preservation League

Hereford Inlet Lighthouse Free grounds

111 North Central Avenue, North Wildwood

The oldest structure in the Wildwoods, guiding boats through Hereford Inlet since 1874. Its English-style gardens hold more than 200 plant varieties and run right up to the seawall.

Good to know: the gardens are free. Lighthouse tours run in season.

Lighthouse hours and tours

Day tripsCape May and beyond.

Head south to the tip of New Jersey for Victorian streets, a lighthouse climb and the island’s best sunset, or inland to one of the best free zoos in the country.

Historic Cape May

Cape May

Gingerbread Victorian houses, a beautiful beach and the Washington Street Mall, an outdoor pedestrian mall full of independent shops and restaurants.

Cape May Lighthouse

Cape May Point State Park

Climb all 199 steps for views across the Delaware Bay and the Atlantic, then walk the state park’s nature trails below.

Good to know: there’s a fee to climb. Watch for special full-moon evening climbs.

Sunset Beach flag ceremony Free

Sunset Boulevard, Cape May Point

Every summer evening since the 1970s, the flag is lowered at sunset in honor of a U.S. veteran. Stay after to hunt for Cape May diamonds on the sand and spot the old concrete ship offshore.

Good to know: it’s popular, so arrive a little before sunset for parking.

Sunset Beach

Cape May County Park and Zoo Free

Cape May Court House

More than 550 animals from about 250 species on 85 acres, including a big African savanna with giraffes and zebras, plus playgrounds and picnic areas. Ranked the fifth-best free attraction in the U.S. by USA Today readers in 2026.

Good to know: admission and parking are free, and it’s open daily year-round except Christmas.
