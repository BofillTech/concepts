The Legend

Built stone by stone, on purpose.

Schweitzer Castle is not a themed rental dressed up for photographs — it is quarried stone, 22 inches thick, hand-laid over years above Sandpoint, Idaho. Its owner built it to last, with hydronic-heated floors, hand-carved statues and armor, mounted swords, and stained-glass windows collected alongside genuine antiques from the 15th and 16th centuries.

Next door stands its companion, Château de Melusine — a smaller stone chateau with a conical turret, built in the same spirit. Together the two castles share a mountainside, a mailing address, and absolutely nothing in common with the hotel down the road.

Guests have called it a real, literal castle — heated floors and all — not a facade built to look like one.

The Crossing

Yes, there really is a moat.

Guests walk across an actual moat to reach the Castle's front door — the kind of detail that either delights you immediately or convinces you that you've come to the wrong address. You haven't. Beyond it: 54 stone stairs climb to the top-floor bedroom and its tower views, so pack for a mountain, not a mailbox.

  • Ski-in / ski-out access to Schweitzer Mountain Resort — expert terrain, direct from the property
  • A 4-wheel-drive vehicle is required from October 31 through April 30
  • Powder Hound Pizza sits about a mile down the road
  • Downtown Sandpoint, City Beach Park and Lake Pend Oreille are 13 miles away

Schweitzer Castle

Schweitzer Castle

Smart TVs, WiFi, and a balcony with lake and mountain views, spread across four levels that blend hand-laid stone with modern comfort. The tower bedroom sits atop 54 stairs and rewards the climb with 360° views. No pets; not recommended for young children given the antiques throughout.

Château de Melusine

Château de Melusine

2 bedSleeps 83 levelsDog-friendly

The great hall's vaulted ceiling, stained-glass kitchenette windows, and 50-foot wood-burning fireplace sit over hydronic-heated Idaho quartzite floors. A private, enclosed hot tub room looks out over the slopes. Up to two dogs welcome; an EV charger is included.

Inside the Stone

A hearth built for banquets, not just s'mores.

Every room carries the same conviction as the walls around it. A 50-foot wood-burning fireplace anchors the great hall. Hydronic-heat floors of Idaho quartzite run underfoot through both castles. Curved stone staircases connect levels that were built, not renovated, to feel medieval.

  • Hand-carved statues, armor, and mounted swords throughout, alongside 15th- and 16th-century antiques
  • Colorful stained-glass windows, several original to the build
  • A tower with 360° views of Schweitzer Mountain and Lake Pend Oreille
  • Hydronic-heated Idaho quartzite floors throughout — warm stone, even in a Sandpoint winter
